# Launch Plan

> Market deliberately deferred; first region profiles are **PK + US**
> ([`../specs/REGION_PROFILES.md`](../specs/REGION_PROFILES.md)). Run the pilot in one
> tightly-defined geography, then expand on evidence — not on ambition.

## Days 1–30 — validate the problem

No significant ad spend.

- Interview 30–50 potential users across segments
- Speak to ≥5 university or community representatives
- Interview people with safety-product or emergency-response experience
- Test the Safe Journey prototype with real users
- Identify the most confusing permission and onboarding steps
- Landing page + waitlist; recruit pilot users

**Ask:** When do you feel least comfortable travelling alone? What do you do now? Who
would you trust with your location? Would you use a journey check-in? What would stop
you installing a safety app? Would you pay for monitoring or family features? What
privacy concern would stop you?

**Do not ask only whether people like the idea — observe whether they use it.**

## Days 31–60 — controlled pilot

100–300 users, one geography.

Measure: onboarding completion · **contact consent completion** · journey starts and
completions · check-in handling · **notification delivery and acknowledgement** ·
battery · permission failures · confusing alerts · **accidental SOS rate** · Practice
Mode completion.

Run simulated emergencies **with informed participants only**. Never create a live
emergency that could involve real responders — Practice Mode (RES-18) is the supported
mechanism.

Objective: learn whether the app is **dependable and understandable**. Not growth.

## Days 61–90 — public launch

**Only if the critical workflows meet [`../engineering/RELEASE_CRITERIA.md`](../engineering/RELEASE_CRITERIA.md).**

Channels: university partnerships · student ambassadors · safety and travel creators ·
organic short-form video · referral · travel and accommodation partners · PR and
product-launch communities.

> A small successful launch is worth more than a large campaign that exposes
> unreliable emergency workflows. A safety app gets one reputation.

## Scaling stages — planning milestones, not forecasts

| Stage         | Users    | Objective                    | Focus                             |
| ------------- | -------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Validation    | 100–500  | Prove core workflows         | SOS, journeys, check-ins          |
| Early launch  | 500–5k   | Establish repeat use         | Reliability, onboarding, referral |
| PMF           | 5k–25k   | Find the sustainable segment | Premium, retention                |
| Regional      | 25k–100k | Expand distribution          | Partnerships, localisation        |
| International | 100k–1M  | Repeat the model             | Regional ops, compliance, scale   |

**Growth follows reliability, never the reverse.**

## Public-sector posture

Where official safety apps already exist, position as **complementary**, never as an
alternative official dispatch service. Explore partnership; until an agreement exists,
describe emergency-service integration as a future capability.
