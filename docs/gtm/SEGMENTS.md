# Segments and Channels

## Launch focus — two related audiences

1. **Young adults and students who travel independently**
2. **Families and friends who want reliable check-ins**

They share one use case: _one person travels, another wants to know they arrived
safely._ That gives a single product story and a built-in referral mechanism.

## Full segment map

| Segment                     | Need                          | Channel                                     | Monetisation         |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| University students         | Safe journeys, check-ins      | Universities, societies, campus ambassadors | Institutional + Plus |
| Women and solo commuters    | Discreet SOS, trusted sharing | Creators, NGOs, community groups            | Freemium → Plus      |
| Families                    | Coordination                  | Referral, family content                    | Family               |
| Solo travellers             | Journey sharing               | Agencies, hotels, travel creators           | Plus                 |
| Corporate / lone workers    | Lone-worker protection        | Direct B2B                                  | Per-employee         |
| Outdoor enthusiasts         | Activity check-ins            | Cycling, hiking, running communities        | Plus                 |
| Older adults and caregivers | Check-ins, help coordination  | Care organisations, families                | Family / care        |

## Institutional constraint — the one that builds trust

A university or employer subscription **never** grants continuous visibility into
personal journeys. They receive only what the agreed safety workflow requires.

Say this explicitly in institutional sales. It is a differentiator, not a limitation —
and the first thing a student union will ask.

## Growth loop

Install → invite a contact → contact receives a **journey link** → experiences the
product with **no install** → installs to build their own circle → both use journeys.

## Not targeting women exclusively

Women experiencing harassment are an important segment and shape the discreet and
coercion-aware design. But platform positioning across commuters, students, travellers,
cyclists, lone workers and caregivers gives multiple acquisition channels while the
pilot stays focused.
