# Positioning

```
Lumo — Your Light in the Dark.
```

> **A safety network, not a panic button.**
> Your everyday safety companion, with intelligent assistance when it matters most.

## Why not market this as an AI app

People do not install a safety app because it uses an advanced model. They install it
because they want confidence about a journey, to protect someone they care about, or a
reliable way to ask for help.

Sell the **outcome**: peace of mind, and a clear plan when something goes wrong.

## Three messages

**A — Everyday confidence.** _"Wherever you're going, someone you trust can stay
connected."_ → journeys, check-ins, commuting, travel.

**B — Help when it matters.** _"One place to start when you need help."_ → SOS,
contacts, incident workflow.

**C — Safety that respects privacy.** _"Your safety. Your people. Your control."_ →
privacy-conscious users and families. The claim an ad-funded competitor cannot make.

## Campaign — "Never Walk Alone, Even When You Do"

Connection, not fear. One problem, one workflow, one outcome per piece:

> A student leaves campus at night → starts a Safe Journey and shares it with a friend
> → the friend follows an authorised link in a browser → the student arrives and ends
> the session. _"A little peace of mind goes a long way. Meet Lumo."_

These are demonstrations, **not** evidence of emergency-service integration or a
guarantee that every feature works on every device — and must be labelled as such.

## Prohibited claims

Checked by the `emergency-review` skill against
[`../engineering/PLATFORM_CAPABILITY_MATRIX.md`](../engineering/PLATFORM_CAPABILITY_MATRIX.md):

- ❌ "Police alerted instantly" — unless a signed integration delivered it
- ❌ "AI that detects every attack" / any unmeasured accuracy figure
- ❌ "Your evidence can never be deleted"
- ❌ "Works everywhere, offline" — an offline phone cannot deliver a remote alert
- ❌ "Court-admissible evidence"
- ❌ Crash detection as equivalent to a validated automotive system
- ❌ Continuous background listening (impossible on iOS)
- ❌ Automatic silent SMS (impossible on both platforms)
- ❌ Frightening advertising or exaggerated crime statistics

## Credibility rules

1. Never claim a feature that is a mock — [`../audit/FEATURES.md`](../audit/FEATURES.md) is the source of truth
2. The SOS screen must not imply contact until it happened, and must show real per-contact state
3. State regional and device limits plainly, as Google does for crash detection
4. **Publish the failure modes** — no signal, phone off, permission denied. Users deserve to know the edges of what they are trusting

A safety app that over-promises is a legal problem as well as an ethical one.
